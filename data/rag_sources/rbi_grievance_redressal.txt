RESERVE BANK — INTEGRATED OMBUDSMAN SCHEME, 2021 (RB-IOS 2021)
AND
RBI DIRECTIONS ON GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL FOR LENDING INSTITUTIONS
(Effective November 12, 2021, Updated: April 2024)

CHAPTER I: SCOPE AND APPLICABILITY

1.1 The Reserve Bank Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (RB-IOS) 2021 consolidates the previously separate ombudsman schemes for banks, NBFCs, and digital transactions into a single unified framework.

1.2 The scheme covers complaints against all entities regulated by RBI including:
(a) Scheduled commercial banks (public, private, foreign)
(b) Regional rural banks and local area banks
(c) Small finance banks and payment banks
(d) Co-operative banks
(e) NBFCs (including HFCs and NBFC-MFIs)
(f) System participants in payment systems (NPCI, card networks, etc.)
(g) Credit Information Companies

CHAPTER II: GROUNDS FOR COMPLAINT

2.1 A complainant may file a complaint on any of the following grounds:
(a) Non-observance of RBI directions on lending practices
(b) Non-adherence to Fair Practices Code
(c) Delay or refusal to provide agreed services
(d) Levying of charges without adequate prior notice
(e) Non-adherence to directions on digital lending
(f) Deficiency in services relating to loans and advances including:
    - Non-return of documents/securities after loan closure
    - Incorrect application of interest rates
    - Harassment by recovery agents
    - Non-issuance of NOC after loan settlement

2.2 CREDIT SCORING COMPLAINTS: The following are valid grounds for complaint related to credit scoring:
(a) Failure to provide explanation of score factors upon request
(b) Use of protected characteristics in scoring
(c) Refusal to accept or process data disputes
(d) Delayed reassessment after data correction
(e) Non-disclosure of data sources used in scoring
(f) Retaining scoring data beyond the consented period

CHAPTER III: COMPLAINT FILING PROCESS

3.1 PREREQUISITE: Before approaching the Ombudsman, the complainant must:
(a) First file a complaint with the regulated entity
(b) Wait for 30 days for resolution (or receive a final rejection)
(c) If unsatisfied with the resolution or no response within 30 days, the complainant can file with the Ombudsman

3.2 FILING CHANNELS:
(a) Online portal: https://cms.rbi.org.in
(b) Physical complaint: by mail to the Centralised Receipt and Processing Centre (CRPC) at RBI, Chandigarh
(c) Email to: CRPC@rbi.org.in
(d) Toll-free helpline: 14448

3.3 TIME LIMIT: A complaint must be filed within 1 year from the date of:
(a) The final rejection of the complaint by the regulated entity, or
(b) The expiry of 30 days from the date of filing the complaint with the regulated entity (if no response received)

CHAPTER IV: INVESTIGATION AND RESOLUTION

4.1 The Ombudsman shall attempt to promote settlement of the complaint by conciliation within 30 days of receipt.

4.2 If conciliation fails, the Ombudsman may pass an Award directing the regulated entity to:
(a) Perform specific actions to redress the complaint
(b) Pay compensation up to Rs. 20 lakh (or actual loss, whichever is lower)
(c) For complaints involving systemic issues, the Ombudsman may recommend regulatory action

4.3 APPEALS: Either party may appeal the Ombudsman's decision to the Appellate Authority (Executive Director, RBI) within 30 days.

CHAPTER V: INTERNAL GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL MECHANISM

5.1 Every RE must have a Board-approved grievance redressal policy.

5.2 GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL OFFICER (GRO): Every RE must designate a senior official as the Grievance Redressal Officer. The GRO's details must be:
(a) Displayed prominently at all branches
(b) Published on the RE's website
(c) Included in all loan sanction letters
(d) Available in the mobile app/DLA

5.3 INTERNAL ESCALATION: The RE must have a multi-tier escalation mechanism:
(a) Level 1: Branch/Customer service team (resolution within 7 days)
(b) Level 2: Grievance Redressal Officer (resolution within 15 days)
(c) Level 3: Principal Nodal Officer (resolution within 30 days)
(d) Level 4: Board-level committee (for systemic or policy issues)

5.4 TRACKING: Every complaint must be assigned a unique tracking number and the complainant must be kept informed of progress.

CHAPTER VI: COMPENSATION FRAMEWORK

6.1 RBI's Integrated Ombudsman Scheme provides for compensation in case of:
(a) Failure to provide contracted services
(b) Loss due to deficiency in services
(c) Mental agony and harassment (up to Rs. 1 lakh)

6.2 For alternate credit scoring complaints, compensation may be awarded for:
(a) Loss of opportunity due to incorrect scoring (documented cases only)
(b) Delayed reassessment causing financial loss
(c) Privacy violations in data handling

6.3 SYSTEMIC CORRECTIONS: Where the Ombudsman identifies systemic deficiencies, the RE must:
(a) Implement corrective measures within 90 days
(b) Report compliance to the Ombudsman
(c) The corrective measures may include changes to scoring methodology, data handling processes, or consent management procedures
