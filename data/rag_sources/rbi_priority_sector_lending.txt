RBI MASTER DIRECTION ON PRIORITY SECTOR LENDING (PSL)
(RBI/FIDD/2020-21/72, Updated: March 2024)

CHAPTER I: TARGETS AND CLASSIFICATION

1.1 Priority Sector Lending (PSL) refers to lending to specific sectors identified by the RBI as essential for economic and social development. Banks must lend a prescribed percentage of their Adjusted Net Bank Credit (ANBC) to priority sectors.

1.2 PSL TARGETS:
(a) Domestic scheduled commercial banks: 40% of ANBC
(b) Foreign banks with 20 or more branches: 40% of ANBC
(c) Foreign banks with less than 20 branches: 40% of ANBC (phased implementation)
(d) Regional Rural Banks and Small Finance Banks: 75% of ANBC

1.3 SUB-TARGETS:
(a) Agriculture: 18% of ANBC
(b) Micro Enterprises: 7.5% of ANBC
(c) Weaker Sections: 12% of ANBC (domestic banks), 12% (foreign banks with 20+ branches)

CHAPTER II: CATEGORIES OF PRIORITY SECTOR

2.1 AGRICULTURE: Includes farm credit (crop loans, land development), agriculture infrastructure (irrigation, warehouses), and ancillary activities (dairy, fishery, beekeeping).

2.2 MICRO, SMALL AND MEDIUM ENTERPRISES (MSMEs):
(a) Micro Enterprise: Investment up to Rs. 1 crore and turnover up to Rs. 5 crore
(b) Small Enterprise: Investment up to Rs. 10 crore and turnover up to Rs. 50 crore
(c) Medium Enterprise: Investment up to Rs. 50 crore and turnover up to Rs. 250 crore

2.3 For PSL classification, the MSME registration on the Udyam portal is the primary identification mechanism.

2.4 EDUCATION LOANS: Loans to individuals for educational purposes including vocational education, up to Rs. 20 lakh.

2.5 HOUSING LOANS: Loans up to Rs. 35 lakh in metropolitan centres and Rs. 25 lakh in other centres for purchase/construction of dwelling units.

2.6 SOCIAL INFRASTRUCTURE: Loans up to Rs. 5 crore for schools, healthcare facilities, sanitation, clean energy, etc.

2.7 RENEWABLE ENERGY: Loans for solar, biomass-based power generators, wind mills, micro-hydel plants, and non-conventional energy.

2.8 WEAKER SECTIONS: Includes Small and Marginal Farmers, Artisans, SC/ST borrowers, SHG/JLG members, DRI scheme borrowers, minority communities, individual women beneficiaries.

CHAPTER III: ALTERNATE CREDIT ASSESSMENT FOR PSL BORROWERS

3.1 CREDIT-INVISIBLE PSL BORROWERS: A significant proportion of PSL-eligible borrowers are credit-invisible, particularly:
(a) First-time farmers taking crop loans
(b) Micro-enterprise owners operating in the informal economy
(c) Weaker section borrowers with no formal banking history
(d) Women beneficiaries in rural areas

3.2 ALTERNATE SCORING FOR MSME LENDING: Banks may use alternate credit assessment models for MSME lending provided:
(a) The model incorporates GST filing data where available
(b) Bank account transaction analysis (AA framework or statement analysis) is included
(c) The model does not substitute for due diligence requirements
(d) Scoring outcomes are explainable to the borrower

3.3 DIGITAL FOOTPRINT SCORING: For micro-enterprise borrowers, digital footprint data may include:
(a) UPI transaction volume and consistency
(b) QR code payment patterns
(c) E-commerce seller metrics (order volume, return rates, ratings)
(d) Utility and telecom payment regularity

3.4 COLLATERAL-FREE LENDING: Loans to MSMEs up to Rs. 10 lakh may be extended without collateral under the Mudra scheme. Alternate credit scoring can support collateral-free lending decisions by providing creditworthiness assessment where traditional credit history is absent.

CHAPTER IV: REPORTING AND COMPLIANCE

4.1 Banks must report PSL achievement to RBI on a quarterly basis. Shortfall in PSL targets must be deposited with NABARD/SIDBI/NHB.

4.2 PSL certificates (PSLCs) can be traded on the e-Kuber platform to meet sub-target shortfalls.

4.3 For loans originated through alternate credit assessment, the bank must maintain records of:
(a) The scoring methodology used
(b) Data sources consulted
(c) The applicant's consent for each data source
(d) Audit trail of the credit decision

CHAPTER V: INTEREST SUBVENTION AND GOVERNMENT SCHEMES

5.1 Interest subvention of 2% is available for short-term crop loans up to Rs. 3 lakh. An additional 3% subvention is provided for prompt repayment.

5.2 Stand-Up India scheme provides loans between Rs. 10 lakh and Rs. 1 crore to SC/ST and women entrepreneurs for greenfield enterprises.

5.3 Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana (PMMY) provides loans up to Rs. 10 lakh for non-farm micro/small enterprises under three categories:
(a) Shishu: up to Rs. 50,000
(b) Kishore: Rs. 50,001 to Rs. 5 lakh
(c) Tarun: Rs. 5,00,001 to Rs. 10 lakh
