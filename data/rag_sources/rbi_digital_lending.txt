RBI GUIDELINES ON DIGITAL LENDING
(RBI/2022-23/111 DOR.CRE.REC.66/21.07.001/2022-23, September 2, 2022)

CHAPTER I: SCOPE AND APPLICABILITY

1.1 These guidelines apply to all commercial banks, co-operative banks, and NBFCs (including HFCs) regulated by the RBI and their Lending Service Providers (LSPs) and Digital Lending Apps (DLAs).

1.2 "Digital lending" means a remote and automated lending process, involving minimal human interaction, using digital technologies in the processes of credit intermediation including credit assessment, loan approval, disbursement, recovery and associated customer service.

1.3 A "Lending Service Provider" (LSP) means an agent of a Regulated Entity (RE) who carries out, in customer interaction, one or more of lender's functions in digital lending in conformity with extant outsourcing guidelines issued by the Reserve Bank.

1.4 A "Digital Lending App" (DLA) means a mobile or web-based application with a user interface that facilitates digital lending activities of a RE or an LSP.

CHAPTER II: CUSTOMER PROTECTION AND CONDUCT

2.1 DISBURSEMENT AND SERVICING: All loan disbursals and repayments shall be executed only between the bank accounts of the borrower and the RE without any pass-through or pool account of the LSP.

2.2 FEE TRANSPARENCY: Any fees, charges, or interest payable by the borrower, including penal interest or charges for late payment, must be disclosed to the borrower as part of the loan agreement. The all-inclusive cost of digital loans in the form of Annual Percentage Rate (APR) must be disclosed.

2.3 KEY FACT STATEMENT (KFS): A standardized Key Fact Statement (KFS) must be provided to the borrower before executing the loan contract. The KFS shall include:
(a) Details of the APR (Annual Percentage Rate)
(b) Recovery mechanism
(c) Details of grievance redressal officer designated specifically for digital lending
(d) Cooling-off/look-up period during which a borrower can exit the digital loan

2.4 COOLING-OFF PERIOD: The borrower shall be given a cooling-off period (also called look-up period) to exit the digital loan by paying the principal and the proportionate APR without any penalty. The Board of each RE shall formulate a policy on the look-up period. This period must be clearly communicated before loan disbursement.

2.5 DATA PRIVACY: DLAs shall not access mobile phone resources like file storage, media, contact list, call logs, telephony functions, etc. One-time access can be taken for camera, microphone, and location only with explicit consent. Consent must be need-based and the purpose of data access must be clearly stated.

2.6 DATA STORAGE: Data collected by DLAs should be need-based, with prior and explicit consent of the borrower, and shall be stored only in servers located in India. All data shall be deleted upon loan closure or withdrawal of consent, subject to regulatory retention requirements.

CHAPTER III: TECHNOLOGY AND INFRASTRUCTURE

3.1 AUTOMATED DECISION-MAKING: Where automated credit decision systems are used, the RE must ensure:
(a) The algorithms do not result in discrimination against any class of borrowers
(b) Human oversight mechanisms exist for automated decisions
(c) The borrower has the right to seek human review of automated decisions

3.2 REPORTING TO CREDIT INFORMATION COMPANIES (CICs): REs shall ensure that any lending done through DLAs or LSPs is reported to CICs irrespective of its nature or tenure.

3.3 DIGITAL LENDING APPS GOVERNANCE: Only the DLAs of the REs and their LSPs can be deployed for servicing digital loans. Any other lending through unregulated apps is impermissible.

CHAPTER IV: FIRST LOSS DEFAULT GUARANTEE (FLDG)

4.1 FLDG is a contractual arrangement between an RE and an LSP or between two REs whereby the LSP or other RE guarantees to compensate the lending RE in case of default up to a certain percentage of the loan portfolio.

4.2 The FLDG arrangement shall be capped at 5% of the amount of that loan portfolio. Any FLDG arrangement exceeding 5% is not permitted.

4.3 The FLDG arrangement must be backed by a cash deposit, fixed deposit, or bank guarantee with a scheduled commercial bank. No other forms of FLDG are permissible.

4.4 The RE must perform its own credit appraisal. The existence of FLDG does not absolve the RE of its responsibilities for credit assessment.

CHAPTER V: GRIEVANCE REDRESSAL

5.1 REs shall ensure that they and their LSPs/DLAs have a suitable nodal grievance redressal officer to deal with FinTech/digital lending related complaints.

5.2 The details of the grievance redressal officer must be prominently displayed on the DLA, the website of the RE and the LSP.

5.3 If the complaint is not resolved within 30 days, the borrower can escalate the matter to the RBI under the Reserve Bank Integrated Ombudsman Scheme (RB-IOS 2021).

CHAPTER VI: LSP AND DLA COMPLIANCE

6.1 REs shall publish the list of their LSPs and DLAs on their websites along with the details of the activities for which they have been engaged.

6.2 All product-level information shall be available on the website of the RE and must not be accessible only through the DLA/LSP.

6.3 The loan agreement must clearly state the identity of the RE and the borrower must be made aware that they are entering into a contract with the RE and not the LSP or DLA.

6.4 The RE shall implement adequate supervision and audit mechanisms for the LSPs and DLAs engaged by them. LSPs shall not be involved in any decision related to the credit assessment or loan sanctioning process.
