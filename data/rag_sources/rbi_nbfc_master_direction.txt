RBI MASTER DIRECTION — NON-BANKING FINANCIAL COMPANY — SCALE BASED REGULATION
(RBI/DoR/2023-24/105, Updated: October 2023)

CHAPTER I: CLASSIFICATION AND APPLICABILITY

1.1 NBFCs are classified into four layers under the Scale Based Regulation (SBR) framework:
(a) NBFC-Base Layer (NBFC-BL): NBFCs with asset size below Rs. 1,000 crore
(b) NBFC-Middle Layer (NBFC-ML): NBFCs with asset size between Rs. 1,000 crore and Rs. 5,000 crore, deposit-taking NBFCs, and specific categories
(c) NBFC-Upper Layer (NBFC-UL): Top 10 NBFCs by asset size and others identified by RBI based on systemic risk parameters
(d) NBFC-Top Layer (NBFC-TL): NBFCs identified as posing extreme systemic risk

1.2 NBFC-INVESTMENT AND CREDIT COMPANIES (NBFC-ICC): This is the most common category of NBFC engaged in lending and investment activities. Most fintech lenders and alternate credit scoring platforms are associated with or operate as NBFC-ICCs.

CHAPTER II: CAPITAL ADEQUACY

2.1 MINIMUM NET OWNED FUND (NOF):
(a) NBFC-ICC: Rs. 10 crore (for new registrations after April 2022)
(b) NBFC-MFI: Rs. 7 crore (Rs. 10 crore for those in North-Eastern region)
(c) NBFC-P2P: Rs. 2 crore

2.2 CAPITAL TO RISK-WEIGHTED ASSETS RATIO (CRAR):
(a) NBFC-BL: Minimum 15%
(b) NBFC-ML: Minimum 15%
(c) NBFC-UL: Minimum 15% with common equity Tier 1 of at least 9%

CHAPTER III: INCOME RECOGNITION, ASSET CLASSIFICATION, AND PROVISIONING (IRACP)

3.1 INCOME RECOGNITION: Income on non-performing assets (NPAs) shall not be recognized on accrual basis. Interest income on NPAs can be recognized only when actually received.

3.2 NPA CLASSIFICATION: An asset is classified as NPA if:
(a) Interest or instalment of principal remains overdue for more than 90 days in respect of term loans
(b) The account remains out of order for more than 90 days in respect of overdraft/cash credit accounts

3.3 ASSET CLASSIFICATION CATEGORIES:
(a) Standard Assets: Assets not classified as NPA
(b) Sub-standard Assets: Assets that have been NPA for not more than 12 months
(c) Doubtful Assets: Assets that have been NPA for more than 12 months
(d) Loss Assets: Assets where loss has been identified but not fully written off

3.4 PROVISIONING NORMS:
(a) Standard Assets: 0.40% (general)
(b) Sub-standard Assets: 10%
(c) Doubtful Assets: 20% to 100% depending on period
(d) Loss Assets: 100%

CHAPTER IV: FAIR PRACTICES CODE FOR NBFCs

4.1 NBFCs must adopt a Fair Practices Code (FPC) as approved by their Board. The FPC must cover:
(a) Applications for loans and their processing
(b) Loan appraisal and terms/conditions
(c) Disbursement of loans
(d) General provisions regarding rights of borrowers

4.2 LOAN REJECTION: When a loan application is rejected, the NBFC must communicate to the borrower in writing the reasons for rejection. For alternate scoring-based rejections:
(a) The specific score and threshold must be communicated
(b) The primary negative factors must be explained
(c) The applicant must be informed of their right to dispute data inaccuracies

4.3 INTEREST RATE POLICY: NBFCs must have a Board-approved interest rate policy. The rate of interest and the approach for gradations of risk must be disclosed to the borrower at the time of sanction.

4.4 RECOVERY PRACTICES: NBFCs shall not resort to undue harassment for recovery. Recovery agents must follow the Indian Banks' Association guidelines. Night-time calls (7 PM to 7 AM) are prohibited.

CHAPTER V: DIGITAL LENDING SPECIFIC PROVISIONS FOR NBFCs

5.1 NBFCs engaged in digital lending must comply with the RBI Guidelines on Digital Lending (September 2022) in addition to the SBR framework.

5.2 FLDG arrangements with LSPs are subject to the 5% cap as per digital lending guidelines.

5.3 Alternate credit scoring models used by NBFCs must:
(a) Be disclosed to the regulator
(b) Not discriminate on protected characteristics
(c) Be subject to periodic bias audits
(d) Provide explainability to applicants

5.4 OUTSOURCING: NBFCs outsourcing credit assessment to third-party alternate scoring platforms must:
(a) Retain ultimate responsibility for lending decisions
(b) Ensure the scoring platform complies with data privacy regulations
(c) Maintain records of scoring methodology and audit trails
(d) Report outsourced credit assessment arrangements to RBI

CHAPTER VI: MICROFINANCE INSTITUTIONS (NBFC-MFI)

6.1 NBFC-MFIs lend to borrowers with household annual income up to Rs. 3,00,000. For urban borrowers, the threshold is Rs. 3,00,000, and for rural borrowers, Rs. 3,00,000.

6.2 PRICING CAP: The maximum interest rate for microfinance loans shall not exceed the all-inclusive interest rate cap set by the Board of the NBFC-MFI. The margin cap has been removed but the rate must be disclosed upfront.

6.3 HOUSEHOLD INCOME ASSESSMENT: For microfinance lending, income assessment may use alternate methods including:
(a) Self-declaration by the borrower
(b) Assessment by the field officer
(c) Digital income proxies (UPI inflows, mobile recharge patterns)
(d) Community-level income estimation

6.4 OVER-INDEBTEDNESS: Total loan obligations of a microfinance borrower (excluding education and medical loans) shall not exceed 50% of the household income.
